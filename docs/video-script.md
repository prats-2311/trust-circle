# Demo video script

Target length: 2:50, under Devpost's 3-minute limit. One narrator. Every on-screen line below is real copy from the app.

## Recording notes

- Record on the production URL (https://trust-circle.onrender.com), not a dev server, so no dev badge appears. Warm the service first by loading `/api/health` a minute before you start: a sleeping free Render service takes about a minute on its first request.
- Run the `demo reset` workflow from the repo's Actions tab (or record soon after 09:00 UTC) so the demo starts clean.
- Two devices, each with its own sign-in: **Margaret on the iOS Simulator** (iPhone 17 Pro, in Device Hub), **Daniel in the Trust Circle app installed from Chrome on the Mac** (and later Sarah). Browser zoom 100%. Make Daniel's app window about phone width. Use a laptop window at about 1280 px for Sarah's dashboard. Hide the desktop icons before recording.
- For the push shot (1:00), turn on alerts for Daniel once, before the final demo reset (a reset keeps push subscriptions): in the installed Trust Circle app, **Try as Daniel, her grandson**, then **Settings**, **Alerts**, and allow notifications. When Margaret approves in PayPal, a real macOS notification arrives top-right; click it to open Daniel's request. On a real iPhone, add the app to the Home Screen first and turn on alerts there. If no notification arrives within about 10 seconds, open Daniel's circle screen and say "Daniel sees the request" instead.
- The PayPal sandbox buyer login for the approval step is in the private Devpost testing instructions. Don't show the password field being typed, or the sign-in page's autofill list: cut from **Continue, and let Sarah review it** to the approved hold. PayPal's button says "Complete Purchase"; if that screen is shown, keep the [PayPal: AUTHORIZED, not captured] caption on it.
- The Simulator and the Mac each keep their own sign-in. The installed Chrome app shares its sign-in with ordinary Chrome tabs, so don't switch person in a Chrome tab during the shoot. Switch to Sarah only inside the app, after Daniel's beats. macOS must allow notifications for **Google Chrome** (System Settings, Notifications, style Banners) with Focus off, or Daniel's banner won't show.
- The scam message is a screenshot: `docs/video/scam-chat.png`, an invented, unbranded text conversation from +1 (415) 555-0199. Open it full screen for the hook, then add it in **Check a message** (on the Simulator, it is already in Photos). If the result doesn't show "not Daniel's number", paste the text instead (it is in the testing instructions) and keep the screenshot only for the hook.
- Film the message check and **Report this to PayPal** in one take without reloading: a reloaded check shows the plain "This message is very likely a scam" headline instead of "Someone is pretending to be your grandson".
- On the iOS Simulator, **Report this to PayPal** can't open the email draft (no Mail app) and shows "Safari cannot open the page because the address is invalid". The report itself is saved; film only the confirmation note. To show the draft, run the report in Chrome on the Mac with Mail set up, and close the draft without sending it.
- The check's headline is the AI's wording and can differ between runs ("your grandson", "Grandma's family member"). Captions must match what is on screen; pasting the text instead of the screenshot usually gives "your grandson".
- A demo reset or a new deploy can sign a window out. Before each take, check Daniel's window still says "You look out for Margaret"; if it shows the landing page, tap **Try as Daniel, her grandson** again (his alerts stay on).
- Use only invented names, numbers and companies, as in the script (`daniel.newphone@example.com`, `+1 (415) 555-0199`).
- No third-party trademarks beyond PayPal's own sandbox UI, which the project integrates. No logos of banks or other brands. No music unless it is licensed for this use or you made it; silence or room tone is fine.
- Upload to YouTube as **Public** and check the length is under 3:00.
- Before recording, read every line aloud at a calm pace and time it. A beat that overruns gets trimmed, not rushed.
- The backup beat (0:52) is narrated over Margaret's waiting screen: the safe-word step itself only appears after 10 minutes without Daniel's answer, so it isn't filmed. A right word never releases a high-risk payment by itself, so don't say it "approves" anything.
- Press **Review recent payments** once before the Sarah take: the first press after a quiet spell can fall back to Trust Circle's own checks.
- Record each beat separately and cut them together. An AI voiceover is allowed (organisers' announcement of 2 Nov). Upload early: YouTube processing can take hours.
- Put a caption on every beat: judges often watch on mute. The "PayPal:" captions name the real PayPal state at that moment; the "PayPal says" column on Sarah's dashboard shows the same states, so they can be checked on screen.
- For the genuine beat (1:30), use a second payment to a different, invented payee (for example `rent@harbourlets.example`, $800, "My grandson Daniel asked me to pay his rent deposit"). A new payee, an unusual amount and over the circle limit is high risk on the rules alone, so it is held and Daniel is asked. If it isn't held, re-take with a larger amount. Don't reuse `daniel.newphone@example.com`: after the first beat it is on the scam list.
